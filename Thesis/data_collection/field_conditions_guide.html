Palay field conditions to look for

Illustration (not a photo): one paddy seen from the drone, with the kinds of areas worth marking as zones so the data includes Healthy, Moderate and Poor examples.

Brown border = levee (pilapil). Blue-grey = standing water between rice hills. Each dot = one rice hill.
1

Healthy area

Dense, even, dark green canopy; water standing between hills; plants the same height. From above: almost no water or soil visible.

Expected: Healthy · high NDVI
2

Drier corner (less water)

Often the high end of the paddy or far from the water inlet. No standing water, cracked soil, plants paler, leaves rolled or leaf tips dried.

Moderate to Poor · lower NDVI soil moisture low
3

Yellowing patch

Light green to yellow leaves, starting with the older (lower) leaves; plants shorter with fewer tillers. Often a sign of low nitrogen (check with the Leaf Color Chart: reading 2–3).

Moderate · medium NDVI
4

Uneven growth / gaps

Missing hills, patches of open water or bare soil, plants of mixed heights (e.g. pest damage, poor transplanting, flooding damage).

Poor · low NDVI where the gaps are
5

Different fertilizer management

Neighbouring strips or paddies fertilized differently (more / less / later) often show a visible colour difference in a straight line, darker vs. lighter green. Ask the farmer which part got what.

Healthy vs Moderate side by side
Tip: walk the field with the farmer or an agriculturist first, mark each area with stakes and flags, and make each marked area one zone (about 20 × 20 m) with a soil node inside it. Record what you saw in the log so the field rating has a reason.
